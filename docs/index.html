02 / METHODOLOGYEngle–Granger reference ↗

An idea is only as good
as the way you test it.

Pairs trading assumes a relationship can pull back toward its historical range. This project asks where that assumption holds—and where it breaks.

01

Form the pair

Regress log(A) on log(B) using only the first 252 or 504 sessions. Freeze the intercept and hedge ratio. Inspect the formation-period Engle–Granger test.

02

Observe the spread

Calculate a z-score using a rolling window that excludes today's residual. An entry requires |z| above the entry band and below the stop band.

03

Trade a day later

Execute at the next close with 100% gross notional at entry, allocated in a 1:β dollar ratio. Keep shares fixed. Exit on reversion, a stop, or a time limit.

04

Count the friction

Mark both legs to market each day. Charge costs on actual traded notional and borrow on the short leg. Compare against an uncosted 50/50 long-only pair.

Read the assumptions & limitations +

Data & selection

Yahoo Finance adjusted closes, 2020–2024. Five familiar pairs were chosen for illustration, not profitability. This small, surviving universe has selection and survivorship bias. Adjusted prices are a total-return proxy, not executable historical quotes; dividends and corporate actions are not modeled as separate cash flows.

Statistical tests

Cointegration p-values come from statsmodels' Engle–Granger test with a constant and AIC lag selection, on formation log prices only. The test assumes integrated price series; individual unit-root tests are not enforced. P-values are unadjusted for multiple testing. Imported pairs do not receive a cointegration p-value.

Execution & risk

Close-to-close fills assume sufficient liquidity and short availability. Fee + slippage is a fixed rate per traded dollar. Borrow is an assumed annual rate, charged using prior-close short notional and actual calendar-day gaps / 365. No financing, margin calls, taxes, impact, or cash interest. A z-score stop is delayed to the next close and does not cap losses.

Evaluation

Sharpe uses daily marked equity returns, a zero risk-free rate, and √252 annualization. CAGR uses 252 sessions per year. Win rate counts closed trades. Final positions are forcibly closed at sample end with costs. Parameter changes reuse the test period; reserve a new untouched period before drawing predictive conclusions.

03 / RESEARCH NOTESWORK IN PROGRESS

What I'm investigating.

A student project connecting time-series statistics, portfolio accounting, and software you can inspect.

01

Correlation isn't a trading rule.

Two assets can move together and still drift apart for years. The formation test and the later trade outcomes answer different questions.

02

Costs matter more than the clean chart.

Try increasing costs from 5 to 20 bps. Compare the net return, turnover, and average holding period before judging a strategy.

03

Next experiment: stability over time.

The current model freezes one formation estimate. A future extension would refit on a walk-forward schedule and compare results across separate holdout periods.
